Worth knowing, beyond what's animated here
  • Every entry carries the term it was created in, shown as the number after each command. This is what makes the consistency check possible: AppendEntries includes the term of the entry immediately before the new ones, and a follower only accepts if its own log agrees on that (index, term) pair -- agreeing on the index alone isn't enough, the term has to match too.
  • Click "Seed F1 with a Conflicting Entry," then replicate to F1. The first attempt fails the consistency check (logged as a mismatch); the leader backs off one entry and retries; once it finds a point both logs agree on, it truncates everything after that point on F1 and overwrites it with its own authoritative entries. This backtrack-then-overwrite is the real mechanism -- not a simplification -- and it's the same "leader's log wins" principle already shown a different way in the :doc:`Kafka replication widget ` (which truncates a rejoining replica's log wholesale rather than via this index-by-index negotiation).
  • An entry only needs a majority, not everyone. With 3 nodes, the leader plus ONE follower is already a majority -- watch commitIndex advance the moment you replicate to just F1, without ever touching F2. F2 can stay arbitrarily far behind without blocking progress, as long as enough other nodes are caught up.
  • There's one safety rule this widget's model enforces but doesn't have its own dedicated demo for: a leader can only commit an entry directly if it's from the leader's own current term -- an older entry that happens to reach a majority via a newer leader's replication only becomes committed indirectly, once a current-term entry after it also commits. This guards against a rare but real scenario (Raft paper, Figure 8) where committing an old-term entry too eagerly could let it be overwritten by a future leader that never saw it.
