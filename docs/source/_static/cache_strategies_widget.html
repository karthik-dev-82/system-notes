Worth knowing, beyond what's animated here
  • Cache-aside puts the app in charge of both systems. The cache doesn't know the DB exists -- on a miss, the APP fetches from the DB and populates the cache itself. On a write, the simplest and most common correct approach is to invalidate (delete) the cache entry rather than update it in place, letting the next read repopulate it fresh -- updating both DB and cache from the app risks the two falling out of sync if the write is retried or reordered.
  • Read-through and write-through make the cache itself smart. The app only ever talks to the cache; on a miss, the cache loads from the DB on the app's behalf. Write-through commits synchronously to the DB before acknowledging the app's write, so it's exactly as durable as writing to the DB directly -- just routed through the cache first.
  • Write-back trades durability for write latency. The app's write is acknowledged the instant it hits the cache, before the DB has seen it at all. Click "Simulate Cache Crash" without flushing first, and watch the write vanish -- it was never actually durable, just fast. This is a real, deliberate trade real systems make (write-heavy caches, metrics pipelines) when losing a small window of recent writes is acceptable in exchange for not blocking every write on a synchronous DB round-trip.
  • Try switching strategy mid-session without resetting -- notice the DB and cache contents persist across the switch, because the strategy is a policy for how future operations behave, not a property of the data itself.
