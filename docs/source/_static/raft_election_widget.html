Raft Leader Election: Play With It

5 nodes, discrete ticks instead of real time (message delivery is instant in this model -- the point is the state machine, not network timing). Each follower has its own randomized election timeout; the first one to time out starts a new term and asks for votes.

Worth knowing, beyond what's animated here
  • Randomized timeouts are the whole trick. Every follower's election timeout is picked independently (5-9 ticks here; real Raft implementations typically use 150-300ms). That randomization is what makes it statistically unlikely for two nodes to start an election at the exact same moment -- not a guarantee, just very good odds, which is why "Force a 3-Way Split Vote" exists as a separate button: an unresolved split is real and possible, just rare in practice.
  • A candidate's log has to be at least as up-to-date as a voter's, or the voter refuses -- even in a higher term. This widget's cluster keeps empty logs (log replication is the companion widget below), but the rule is real and load-bearing: it's the entire mechanism behind Raft's Leader Completeness guarantee -- a node that's missing committed data can never win an election over one that has it, because winning requires a majority, and at least one voter in any majority has the committed entry and will refuse a less-caught-up candidate.
  • "Kill 3 of 5" demonstrates the same quorum math as :doc:`the CAP theorem's quorum widget ` -- with only 2 of 5 nodes reachable, no candidate can ever collect the 3 votes a majority requires, so the cluster simply has no leader until enough nodes come back. This isn't a bug or an edge case Raft forgot to handle -- refusing to elect anyone without a real majority is the entire safety guarantee working as designed.
  • Kill the leader and watch a real former-leader come back with "Revive" (via the node's own button) -- it always rejoins as a follower, never resuming its old role. Real Raft never persists "I am the leader" to disk; only the term, vote, and log survive a restart, so a revived node has no way to still believe it's in charge.
