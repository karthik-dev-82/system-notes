Worth knowing, beyond what's animated here
  • This widget's "kind" is a simplification of real caps. Actual GStreamer caps carry pixel format, resolution, framerate, and more -- two "raw video" pads can still fail to negotiate over those details. This widget collapses all of that down to the single layer beginners actually get stuck on: video vs. audio vs. compressed vs. raw vs. an opaque bytestream. videoconvert/audioconvert are real elements doing real work in production pipelines specifically because they absorb that finer-grained mismatch -- that's honestly represented here, just not the pixel-format-level detail underneath it.
  • decodebin's pending state is real, not simulated for effect. A real decodebin has no output pad at all until it has sniffed the stream and figured out what's actually inside -- that's why the callback-based Python examples connect a pad-added handler instead of linking immediately. Try linking straight through a freshly-added decodebin in this widget without probing it first.
  • A queue never fixes a caps mismatch -- it only decouples timing between two elements that already agree on format. This widget models it as a true passthrough for exactly that reason: watch it forward whatever kind was already flowing, unchanged.
  • The reachable-kinds in this widget's palette are intentionally limited: there's no element here that turns video into audio (nor should there be), and no decoder that turns compressed video back into something a raw sink accepts -- a real pipeline for that would need something like avdec_h264, left out here to keep the palette small enough to actually explore.
